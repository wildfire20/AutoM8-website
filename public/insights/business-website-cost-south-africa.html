How much does a business website cost in South Africa?

The honest answer is that a website quote is mainly a scope conversation. A small informational site and a website that handles bookings, payments or accounts are different products, even if both are called “a website”.

Short answer: the cost depends on pages, content, design, integrations, editing needs and ongoing support. Start with the job the website must do, then price the necessary scope. Our business website options and pricing page provide useful starting points, while a custom quote is appropriate for more involved work.

What changes the price of a business website?

When we scope a website, we look beyond the number of pages. A clear brief usually covers the following:

  • Purpose and audience: a brochure site, lead-generation site, booking journey and customer portal have different requirements.
  • Content: supplied copy and photography reduce production work; strategy, writing, editing and image preparation add a separate content task.
  • Design and responsiveness: a considered mobile experience, reusable page layouts and accessible interactions take planning rather than simply shrinking a desktop page.
  • Functionality: forms, search, calculators, bookings, payments, member accounts and dashboards each introduce rules to define and test.
  • Integrations: email, CRM, payment, calendar or stock connections depend on the systems involved and their access requirements.

Website packages versus custom development

A focused business website is often the sensible starting point when the main job is to explain your offer and give prospects a reliable way to contact you. A templated or WordPress-based build can make sense when your team needs familiar content editing and the feature set is well understood.

Custom development becomes more useful when the website must coordinate a process: for example, collecting structured applications, showing different information to different users, or sending data into an operational system. AutoM8 can also phase that work, launching the essential public website before adding a more complex workflow.

Questions to ask before accepting a quote

  1. Which pages and user journeys are included?
  2. Who supplies and owns the copy, images, domain and hosting accounts?
  3. What happens when a visitor submits a form?
  4. Is mobile behaviour included in review and testing?
  5. Which edits can our team make after launch?
  6. What support, updates and future changes are included or excluded?

Do not compare only the headline number

A low initial quote can leave important work—content, mobile refinement, analytics configuration, security updates or post-launch fixes—undefined. A higher quote may include those activities, or it may simply describe a larger solution. Compare the deliverables, assumptions, ownership and handover as well as the price.
